01 Our Sponsor

A community lifeline since 1973

International Community Health Services (ICHS) began as a small donated storefront in Seattle's International District. Fifty years later, it's Washington's largest Asian, Native Hawaiian, and Pacific Islander community health center, and no one is turned away for inability to pay.

Our UW Capstone team redesigned the ICHS website ahead of its 2027 revamp, delivering Figma prototypes, a design system, a coded build, and handoff documentation.

02 The Problem

Good care, hard to find

04 Research

Testing the current site first

Before changing anything, we ran usability tests on the existing website to see how patients look for care and exactly where they get stuck. Five themes came up again and again:

01

Patients value speed and clarity.

02

Task-first navigation works. Shortcuts beat browsing page by page.

03

Language tools need visibility.

04

Dense layouts create overload.

05

Confusing sites increase support calls.

05 User Testing & Validation

Three findings, three fixes

01

Booking next steps were unclear

What we saw

Users didn't know what would happen after clicking "Book Now."

What we changed

The button now reads "Call to Book an Appointment," with the phone number shown, so the next step is obvious.

ICHS can't book appointments online. Patients call the clinic or use MyChart, so the button has to say that plainly.

02

Language tools were hard to find

What we saw

Users, especially non-English speakers, missed the language selector. They didn't recognize the Google Translate icon.

What we changed

A labeled "Select Language" button with a universal globe icon, plus more languages shown with flags and each name in its own script. In the next test round, more users found it and translated the page.

03

Users didn't know where to start

What we saw

Key patient actions were buried several pages deep. Users wanted direct shortcuts.

What we changed

A "Shortcut for Patients" row (Appointments, MyChart, Clinic Services, After Hours Care, Billing) and a step-by-step "Get Started with ICHS" guide for first-time patients.

06 Navigation

A nav bar patients can scan

Before
  • The call center number, which patients rely on to book, was hidden in a corner.
  • The language icon wasn't widely recognized, so people missed it.
  • Services were one long vertical list with no clear grouping.
After
  • The call center number and working hours sit up front in the header.
  • The Services menu runs horizontally in three groups: General & Urgent, Specialized, and Wellness. We used the same three groups everywhere services appear.
07 Find a Location

From a map to the closest clinic

Before
  • A map with a few pins and no sign that it was interactive. To many users it looked like a static picture, and zooming took ⌘ + scroll.
  • Location cards left out clinic hours, so finding them took an extra step. Users who have to dig for simple information often leave the site.
After
  • Users wanted to type an address or city and see the nearest clinic, so we replaced the map with search.
  • Filters sort A–Z or Z–A and narrow by clinic type: medical, dental, mobile health, or school clinic.
  • The closest clinic is featured with its distance. Every card shows the name, address, phone, and hours, with Get Directions and Learn More buttons.
What I'd Change Next

The main search didn't handle ZIP codes, so our engineers added a separate ZIP field. Two search boxes doing similar jobs can confuse patients. Next, I'd merge them so one search bar accepts a ZIP code and returns the closest clinic.

08 Reflection
Capstone Insight
"Designing for accessibility isn't a checklist. It's a commitment to the people who are most often left out."
Test before you redesign
Testing the old site first gave every change a clear reason, and a second round showed whether it worked.
Real constraints shape the design
ICHS can't book online, so the best fix wasn't a booking flow. It was telling patients exactly how to book.
Design and engineering need shared language
The ZIP code field was a quick engineering fix that added a UX cost. Documenting decisions early keeps trade-offs like that visible.
